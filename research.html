Research & Reports

Real research papers and reports from the web, with a short summary and credit to the original authors.

These pages are short educational summaries written from the public sources linked on each card. All credit belongs to the original authors and publishers. We do not copy their work and we include no exploit code or step by step attack instructions. Read the original source for the full details.

Understanding the Mirai Botnet

Paper

A measurement study of the Mirai botnet, built from infected internet-connected devices such as cameras and routers. It explains how the botnet grew, how it was run, and how it was used for very large denial-of-service attacks.

Antonakakis et al. USENIX Security 2017Read the source

Meltdown: Reading Kernel Memory from User Space

Paper

Describes a hardware flaw in many processors that can let one program read memory it should not be able to see, and how operating systems were changed to reduce the risk.

Lipp et al. USENIX Security 2018Read the source

Spectre Attacks: Exploiting Speculative Execution

Paper

Explains a family of processor flaws tied to speculative execution, a speed feature of modern CPUs, and why they can leak data between programs and are hard to remove completely.

Kocher et al. IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2019Read the source

APT1: Exposing One of China's Cyber Espionage Units

Report

A 2013 threat intelligence report that documented a long-running espionage campaign against many organizations and linked it to a specific group. It is an early example of public attribution backed by technical evidence.

Mandiant (now part of Google Cloud), 2013Read the source

Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR)

Report

A yearly report that analyzes real breaches and incidents to show the most common causes and how they change from year to year. Useful for deciding what to defend first.

Verizon, published every yearRead the source

NIST Cybersecurity Framework

Guidance

Free, voluntary guidance from the US standards body for managing cybersecurity risk. It organizes security work into clear functions, so teams of any size can describe where they are and what to improve.

NIST (US National Institute of Standards and Technology)Read the source
